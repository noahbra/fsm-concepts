# Concept 04 research: what would make this site stand out

Date: October 27, 2026. Written before the second Concept 04 was designed, after Noah rejected the first one
("not professional, unclear architecture") and asked for research on what will make the site stand out and how
the concept differs from the first three.

## 1. What the category does

The four reference sites FilmTwist cited (schoolsmarketing.ca, bloomenrollment.com, kreativewebworks.com,
enrollmediagroup.com) and the agencies that appear in the 2026 roundups all run the same page, in nearly the same
order:

| Section | schoolsmarketing.ca | bloomenrollment.com | kreativewebworks.com | enrollmediagroup.com |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Promise hero | "Boost Your School's Admissions Funnel" | "Enrollment is a System... Not a season" | "We Help You Enroll More Students!" | "Custom Partnerships That Drive Enrollment" |
| Pain points | "We know how it feels when..." | "Struggling to Attract the Right Students" | "Why Choose Us" | tailoring copy |
| Process steps | 3 (call, plan, onboard) | 3 (Discovery, Audit, Onboard) | 4 (Listen, Plan, Deliver, Optimize) | none |
| Case cards | 3, no hard figures | 4 with percentages | 2 with figures | linked only |
| Logo wall | 8 schools | 11 logos | badges | dozens, repeated |
| Testimonials | none | 2 | none (Google rating) | 2 |
| CTA | "Book Discovery Call" | "Let's Talk" (Calendly) | "FREE Consultation" | "Connect with EMG" |

Observations:

- Proof is either vague ("record enrollment after one year", their spelling) or unverifiable percentages. None of the four shows a
  campaign as a funnel with leads, applications and enrollments side by side, with the period and a source.
- Every site talks about the agency. None makes the visitor's school the subject of the page.
- Every CTA is a free call. None tells the reader what the call will ask of them.
- Distinctive moves that do exist: Bloom's four-stage model used as navigation; Enroll Media Group's published
  advertising benchmarks and podcasts; Kreative Webworks' free website audit tools. Content and tools, not layout,
  are what separate them.

## 2. What school leaders are told to look for

Agency-written guides, so treat as the category's own advice rather than independent research:

- Ask for school clients you can call, not case studies on a slide, and ask each whether enrollment went up and
  whether they renewed (schoolbranding.agency, "How Schools Should Choose Enrollment Marketing Agencies").
- "A good agency asks for your funnel before they show you anything": monthly inquiries, applications and
  enrollments over several years, and withdrawal reasons (same source).
- "The only number that matters is how many students showed up in September and how much each one cost to get"
  (same source).
- Each lost student is a direct budget hit under per-pupil funding; the pool is shrinking and competition is
  intensifying (Grow Schools, "What the Data Says About K-12 Enrollment Right Now").
- Families research schools online before contacting them; Google removed third-party school reviews from
  business profiles in April 2025, so the school's own visibility matters more (Kreative Webworks trends page).
- Parent decision factors in a January 2026 National School Choice Awareness Foundation survey of 3,960 parents, as
  cited by ScholarSurge: safety and support 61%, social environment 47%, educational approach 39%, academic
  quality 36%, affordability 28%. The original report was not opened; cited figures only.

## 3. What makes agency sites stand out in 2026

From DesignRush, Teamwork and the camgomersall.com reviews of agency sites: immersive proof of craft (Concept 02
already does this), metrics as credibility (dashboards, visual metrics), a clear point of view that makes the
difference obvious in one screen, and craft beneath the visuals (hierarchy, performance, semantics). Minimalism
paired with bold typography is the dominant aesthetic.

## 5. The gap, and the concept

The gap is the visitor's own school. The category's best advice ("ask for the funnel first", "the number that
matters is seats filled and cost per seat") is advice nobody's website acts on.

**Concept 04: Start With Your Number.** The page opens with the one figure every school leader already knows,
the seats they need to fill. Everything that follows is computed from it, using only FilmTwist's three verified
campaigns, and the page ends where a discovery call would begin: with the list of what to bring.

Architecture (one job per section):

1. **Your number.** "How many seats do you need to fill?" One input. A worksheet beneath it fills in the
   applications and inquiries that scale implies, and the media budget range, using the three campaigns' published
   ratios. Shown as ranges, never a point estimate, with the three campaigns plotted on the range.
2. **Where the numbers come from.** The three campaigns as funnels (leads, applications, enrollments where
   reported), with period, cost per lead and source link. Proof as data, not as cards.
3. **What moves each number.** FilmTwist's funnel workflow, each step tied to the worksheet row it changes.
4. **What we ask on the first call.** The data-first stance made literal: the list of numbers to bring, and the
   promise that if a school does not have them, that is the first thing to fix.
5. Services, the invitation, footer.

Guardrails, from the build brief and CONTENT_AND_CLAIMS.md: the worksheet is arithmetic on published, historical
campaign figures and says so on screen; it is labeled "not a forecast, not a guarantee"; only Friendship PCS
reported confirmed enrollments, so the application-to-enrollment yield is shown as that one campaign's figure and
is adjustable by the reader; ROI percentages are not used.

How it differs from 01 to 03: it is the only concept where the reader does something other than read, the only one
that leads with the reader's school instead of the agency, and the only one whose proof section is a comparison of
funnels rather than a set of cards. It is also the concept most aligned with the research on how leaders are told
to choose.
